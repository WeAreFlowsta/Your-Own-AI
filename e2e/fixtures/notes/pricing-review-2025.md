# Pricing review, March 2025

## Why we are looking at the labour rate

The workshop labour rate has been $60 an hour since we opened, and the numbers no longer add up. Rent went up in January, the apprentice scheme costs more than we planned, and the two nearest shops both charge more than we do. This note is the working through of what the labour rate should be.

## What the other shops charge

Corbel Bikes on the high street charges $78 an hour for workshop labour and quotes fixed prices for the common jobs. Wheelhouse, out by the station, charges $72 an hour and is booked two weeks ahead. Neither shop has lost customers over its labour rate as far as anyone can tell. Our labour rate of $60 an hour is the lowest in town by a clear margin.

## What the labour rate has to cover

A mechanic's hour costs us about $34 in wages and on-costs. The workshop's share of rent, power, insurance and tools comes to about $19 for every hour a mechanic is at the stand. That leaves $7 an hour at the current labour rate, before a single slow day. The labour rate has to carry the quiet weeks in winter as well as the busy ones in spring.

## Options for the labour rate

Option one: raise the workshop labour rate to $65 an hour. This covers the rent rise and nothing else. Option two: raise the labour rate to $70 an hour, which is still under both other shops. Option three: go to $75 an hour and match the middle of the market. We talked through each at the Monday meeting.

## Decision on the labour rate

We are setting the workshop labour rate at $70 an hour from April 1, 2025. The labour rate of $70 an hour applies to all workshop jobs. Fixed-price services are recalculated from the $70 labour rate. Signs at the counter and the price list on the website are to be changed the week before. We look at the labour rate again in a year.
